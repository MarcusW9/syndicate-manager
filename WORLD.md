# Lantern Bay: world bible

The setting's canon: history, power, districts and places. Why each piece was chosen is in the decision log in `DESIGN.md`; add a row there whenever this file's canon changes. Crisis hooks waiting to be written up live in `drafts/district-hooks.md`.

- **Name:** Lantern Bay, Chinese name 燈籠灣 (Dang Lung Wan). Final, not a placeholder.
- **Year:** 2057.
- **Supernatural:** none. People believe in luck, ghosts, gods and unlucky numbers, as they do today; the game never confirms any of it.

## History
- **1891, Lantern Course opens:** a racecourse on marshy ground at the bottom of the island's basin, older than most of the city around it. The Basin grew up around it.
- **1970, the Reclamation Boom:** the Docks and Old Town are pushed out into the harbour with dynamite and junk-built seawalls, setting up a gritty, community-run maritime merchant culture.
- **1994, the Great Signal 10:** a super-typhoon wipes out the southern shanty piers and forces an uneasy truce between early street syndicates (the proto-Red Lanterns) and the city's planners.
- **2018, the Neural Integration Act:** pushed by Veridian-Apex BioTech, megacorps fit early neural implants to dockworkers "for occupational safety", causing the first wave of neural-load burnouts. It's why so many working people carry old, glitchy chrome.
- **2035, the Glass Heights Annexation:** corporate landlords bulldoze the upper ridge for climate-controlled bio-towers, cutting the rich off from the damp alleys below.
- **2052, the Grid Blackout:** three weeks without city power, caused by corporate grid-siphoning. Neighbourhoods got through on diesel generators, herbal tea shops and local trust, and crew politics have run on favours ever since.

## Who runs it
- **On paper:** the Municipal District Administration, a board of corporate-appointed bureaucrats behind red tape, safety ordinances and hollow public announcements.
- **In reality:** megacorps and the big factions sit at the top (see the power map). Below them are the neighbourhood powers a small crew can actually reach: Auntie Fung keeps the peace in Old Town with tea and favours in her ledger, as a senior elder of the Brass Anchor Society; Madam Rao holds the Night Market's purse strings; Mr. Szeto reshapes Glass Heights and the tenements below it; the Ferryman owns the night water; the Red Lanterns tax the Docks roads; Ruth Okonkwo brokers the contracts the megacorps pretend don't exist, for Aether-Harbour Logistics' off-books arm.
- **Tongs:** a tong (堂, "hall") is a registered association, much like a guild: on paper a benevolent society with a front room, a ledger and an altar; in practice how anyone below the corps gets organised. The Brass Anchor Society is one of the oldest, and its elders still sit at Auntie Fung's tea tables. Yours started in the room above the noodle shop. A tong's rank is how far its name carries: a back-room tong, a street tong, a district tong, a city tong, and a harbour tong, known on both sides of the water. A *crew* is the people a tong sends on a job.

## Power map
Rumours are street talk: the game never confirms them (no supernatural, and no proof).

**Megacorps**
- **Aether-Harbour Logistics (AHL), the Docks** (sub-surface logistics hubs, container yards). Wants a monopoly on unlogged cross-border shipping and grey-market tech movement. Earns from off-the-books warehousing, corporate espionage and high-margin contraband. Small crews are disposable assets, paid in scrip and dropped the moment they leave a digital trail. Its deniable off-books arm, the one that "officially doesn't exist", is Ruth Okonkwo's employer. *Rumour:* their unmarked cargo lighters never show on harbour radar because they're piloted by backups of executives who died years ago.
- **Veridian-Apex BioTech, Glass Heights.** Wants control of the upper ridge's air and genetics to grow high-yield bio-compounds. Earns from patented crop strains, filtered-oxygen quotas and luxury neural therapies; it pushed the 2018 Neural Integration Act, so it sold the street its implants and now sells the cure. Small crews get cold condescension and work only as deniable muscle in the lower alleys. *Rumour:* the board's longevity tonics are grown on waste runoff from Old Town's tenements.
- **Hoi-Sing Grid & Cable, Old Town and Night Market** (infrastructure nodes). Wants every kilowatt in the city under its meter. Earns from surge pricing, power-theft fines and battery-pack leases. Small crews are petty power thieves, squeezed for bribes or forced into dangerous substation clean-ups. *Rumour:* it triggers micro-blackouts in the Night Market whenever its gambling algorithms need a distraction.

**Triads and gangs**
- **A gang remembers a tong that hurts its business.** With a big enough grudge it sends its people through the door of that tong's hall on its own turf: Red Lantern enforcers in the Docks, Silver Fan collectors in the Night Market and the Basin's Undercroft. Gangs expect settlements; tribute calls it off and eases the grudge. The Brass Anchor Society never raids a tong's hall.
- **The Red Lanterns, the Docks.** Want every land cargo route and protection fee on the southern waterfront. Earn from road tolls on container trucks, dockside rackets and counterfeit hardware. Small crews are junior licensees: pay the toll on time, respect the incense, and they share dock gossip. *Rumour:* their dragon head consults a blind fortune-teller in the container yards before every big hijacking.
- **The Silver Fan Circle, Night Market.** Wants the underground credit economy and the counterfeit luxury trade. Earns from high-interest street loans, gambling parlours and fake designer implants. Small crews are walk-in debtors or runners who clear their tabs with messy collection work. *Rumour:* lose at mahjong to the elders on the third floor and you pay with your biometric keys.
- **The Brass Anchor Society, Old Town.** Wants Old Town left to itself and its businesses safe from corporate buyouts. Earns from dai pai dong protection fees, herbal-shop levies and old-school mediation; Auntie Fung is a senior elder, and her tea shops are its front room. Small crews get warm, suspicious paternalism: prove yourself over a bowl of beef chow fun and they'll back you in a brawl. *Rumour:* they hold handwritten debt ledgers from the 2052 blackout that could ruin any developer trying to buy out Old Town.

**Police**
- **Municipal Harbour Police, 4th Precinct (the "Wet Sleeve" unit), the Docks and the Night Market borders.** Wants steady protection money, low paperwork and no internal-affairs audits. Earns from cuts of Red Lantern smuggling, shaking down night boats and selling confiscated gear. Small crews are walking cash machines: stopped for "neural-load checks" and fined unless they pay in cash or intel. When a district runs hot, the precinct sends a raid through the door of any tong's hall there; only the Wet Sleeve, on its own patch, will sell one off, and never cheaply. Old Town's patrol pairs take small bribes but can't call off a raid, and the Basin's Association security and Glass Heights' Veridian-Apex guards can't be bought at all. *Rumour:* the commander's office has a back door straight into a Red Lantern warehouse.

**Institutions**
- **The Lantern Bay Racing Association, the Basin.** Officially independent: old, wealthy, deep inside the Municipal District Administration and ferociously protective of its name. Runs the racing, licensed betting, the big legal pools, horse registration, jockey licensing and the vets, and presents itself as one of the few things in Lantern Bay that belongs to the whole city, which is roughly true. Wants punters to keep believing a horse can genuinely win or lose, because the betting economy dies the day they stop. Small crews are deniable help when it needs a scandal found, or buried, before race night. *Rumour:* somebody has already crossed the line, and a horse has won a big race running on software.

**Wildcard**
- **The Ghost Circuit, Old Town** (drainage tunnels and abandoned bomb shelters). Wants free city data streams and free, unmonitored neural-load cleansing for street workers. Earns from siphoned corporate data, black-market firmware flashing and decrypted city security bypasses. Small crews are possible allies, or beta-testers for their glitchy home-made neural scrubbers. *Rumour:* they're led by a collective mind formed when five dockworkers' implants fused during the 2052 blackout.

### Foreign commercial interests

Lantern Bay is a port city. Money, machinery and people arrive from elsewhere, and some never quite leave.

Foreign firms finance cargo, supply equipment, maintain regional offices and compete for contracts beneath the three megacorps. They bring their own habits and rivalries, but operating here still means dealing with local landlords, local infrastructure and the people who can make a delivery wait all afternoon.

Foreign does not always mean newly arrived. Some overseas firms have employed local families for generations. Nor does a famous name abroad guarantee power here: a prestigious regional office can still spend the morning waiting for Hoi-Sing to reconnect the electricity.

#### Morrow & Venn
*Glass Heights · Foreign merchant-finance house*

The brass plate outside Venn House has been polished so often that the edges of the letters have softened. Inside, the reception clock ticks above a desk where visitors are still asked to sign their names. There are faster ways to record an arrival. Morrow & Venn prefers this one.

Founded in **Bellweather**, a distant western port city, the firm began with shipping credit and marine insurance. Its Lantern Bay office predates the Reclamation Boom. The company considers this proof that it belongs; people below the ridge consider it proof of how long it has been collecting interest.

It finances voyages, insures cargo, lends against commercial property and administers estates whose owners trusted it more than their relatives. When a shipment disappears, it helps decide who pays. When someone dies, it helps decide who owns what remains.

Its local clerks, surveyors, translators and administrators come from across the city. Visiting partners arrive for important signatures and tend to leave before the disputes become personal.

**What it wants:** to remain the firm people trust with ownership papers, disputed inheritances and losses too expensive to settle with a handshake. Its influence rests on being difficult to replace and harder to surprise.

**How it behaves:** restrained manners, careful records and a strong preference for making someone else accept liability. It can honour a century-old obligation while refusing a desperate person one extra day.

**Ties:** finances and insures legitimate shipping used by AHL and its customers, and considers property lending proposals from developers such as Mr. Szeto. A commercial relationship does not mean it knows what either does out of sight.

**Small crews:** useful for discreet collections, locating signatories and recovering property whose ownership is awkward. A sealed envelope is not proof that the person sending it has a right to what is inside.

**Rumour:** its oldest Lantern Bay strongroom contains ownership documents for land now beneath the harbour, and someone still pays the storage fees.

#### Hang Yuen Systems
*Glass Heights · Foreign industrial technology supplier*

On the Demonstration Floor, a machine reads a parcel, turns it neatly and sends it down the correct belt. It does this all afternoon without a mistake. Behind the staff door, an engineer is drying saltwater out of the version installed at the Docks.

Hang Yuen Systems is headquartered in **Lin Sau**, an inland manufacturing metropolis in the continental market beyond Lantern Bay's mainland hinterland. It sells industrial automation, machine vision and fleet-control software, and its regional office promises that its equipment can work anywhere.

The engineers usually add conditions. The sales team usually leaves them off the slide.

Equipment sales bring the company into the city; software licences, maintenance agreements and integration contracts are supposed to keep it here. It wants long-term supply deals with AHL, Hoi-Sing and other large operators, rather than another year of expensive demonstrations.

Visiting specialists live in serviced accommodation while local subcontractors handle much of the installation. When a schedule slips, the people closest to the equipment tend to receive the blame first.

**What it wants:** to become part of the city's everyday machinery, expensive to remove and too useful to ignore.

**How it behaves:** technically ambitious, commercially impatient and very interested in what counts as a successful acceptance test. Its engineers and account managers do not always give the same answer.

**Ties:** competes for AHL logistics contracts and Hoi-Sing infrastructure work, while renting premises in Veridian-Apex-dominated Glass Heights. It supplies industrial systems, not a replacement for Veridian-Apex's neural and biotech empire.

**Small crews:** useful for retrieving demonstration equipment, finding missing subcontractors and moving sensitive prototypes without advertising a failed trial. If questions are asked, an unauthorised runner remains an unauthorised runner, even when a manager hired them.

**Rumour:** one of its best local demonstrations used a night-shift team correcting the machine's decisions by hand from the room next door.

#### Names and origins

Bellweather and Lin Sau exist beyond the playable city. For now, one is a distant western port and the other an inland manufacturing metropolis; their countries, borders and wider histories remain undefined. So does **Porto Clara**, a casino island a ferry ride down the coast, where the city's gamblers go when Lantern Bay's tables aren't enough.

Local Cantonese names retain Cantonese romanisation. Foreign firms may carry inherited surnames or established brands; Hang Yuen Systems and Lin Sau use Cantonese-rendered names in Lantern Bay's narration, without establishing how people in their home market pronounce them.

## Geography and districts
Layout (canon, 2026-10-25): the harbour splits the city. The island holds Iron Peak on the summit, Glass Heights on the ridge below, the Basin in a bowl between the ridge's lower slopes and Old Town, and Old Town and the Night Market down to the water. The Amber Reach is the crossing over the harbour. The mainland side holds the Docks and, out on the mudflats, the Silt.

Lantern Bay is a dense, vertical city carved into steep granite hills that drop into a crowded, island-studded deep-water harbour. Height and reclamation divide it: the corps' boards live on the summit of Iron Peak, the corporate towers of Glass Heights sit on the ridge below, Old Town and the Night Market fill the neon concrete canyons further down, and the Docks and the Silt sink into industrial rust and man-made mudflats at the waterline. People get around on rusted overhead monorails, grid-siphoned electric trams, diesel ferries across the bay, and a warren of drainage tunnels, footbridges and escalators that never quite reach the top. The rich ride a maglev; everyone else changes trains at the Amber Reach.

The game shows the **English names**; the Chinese names are flavour for district cards and text (as with Hong Kong's Central / 中環, the two needn't translate each other). Chinese names are suggestions; check them with a Cantonese speaker before they ship.

**Open from the start**
- **Old Town** (舊城, Kau Shing). The damp, ancestral heart of the city: herbal tea shops hiding favour ledgers, rusted tenements clinging to the hillside. *Landmarks:* Auntie Fung's tea shop on Alley 4; the Red Incense Shrine of To Dei Gung; the rusted iron stairwell of Tenement Block 9. *Control:* the Brass Anchor Society; Hoi-Sing nodes and Ghost Circuit tunnels underneath. *Police:* sporadic and corrupt; officers come in pairs and take bribes to ignore unlicensed generators. *3am:* damp concrete, burning joss paper, mahjong tiles clacking down wet alleys. *Custom:* a cup of bitter tea on the doorstep at midnight for the ghosts of the 2052 blackout.
- **The Docks** (霧港, Mo Kong, "fog harbour"). A deafening sprawl of automated cranes, diesel and saltwater where freighters unload contraband into the city. *Landmarks:* Pier 12 Gantry Gate; the Ferryman's night-boat landing; the Red Lanterns' Warehouse 7. *Control:* Aether-Harbour Logistics on paper, the Red Lanterns on the tarmac. *Police:* heavy; the Harbour Police "Wet Sleeve" unit runs rackets alongside the gangs. *3am:* amber floodlights in harbour fog, screeching cranes, clanking steel. *Custom:* never whistle past the container stacks, or the water ghosts (水鬼, sui gwai) will trip you into the harbour.
- **The Night Market** (夜光街, Yeh Kwong Kai, "glow street"). A neon maze of dai pai dongs, counterfeit implant clinics and alleys where fortunes go on one hand of mahjong. *Landmarks:* Madam Rao's sari shop, the Silk & Credit Emporium; the Neon Dragon dai pai dong; the third-floor mahjong parlour; the *Hei Lok*, a casino airship moored above the market (below). *Control:* the Silver Fan Circle; Hoi-Sing nodes. *Police:* rarely seen and fully complicit; they take their cut at the noodle stalls and never go near the back-alley operating rooms. *3am:* wok smoke, flickering holo-ads, murmuring gamblers. *Custom:* if a neon sign flickers three times over your head, toss a coin over your left shoulder.
- **Glass Heights** (玻璃山, Po Lei Shan), officially **Apex Ridge** since Veridian-Apex renamed it after the 2035 Annexation; nobody on the street calls it that. Sterile, climate-controlled corporate towers, labs and clinics where the air is filtered and street-level misery is kept out of sight. *Landmarks:* Veridian-Apex Central Tower; the Glass-Bridge Promenade; Executive Spa & Neural Clinic 1. *Control:* Veridian-Apex BioTech. *Police:* none to speak of; security drones and private guards treat anyone without a pass as a biohazard. *3am:* humming air filters, glass monoliths in cold blue light. *Custom:* never press a lift button with a bare index finger, or the lower districts' bad karma rubs off. Tenants, commuters and the work they bring: "Glass Heights: life behind the glass", below.

- **The Basin** (鑊底, Wok Dai, "bottom of the wok"; Cantonese check pending), around **Lantern Course** (燈籠馬場, Dang Lung Ma Cheung). A natural bowl between Old Town and the lower slopes under Glass Heights, its floodlit oval visible from hundreds of cramped balconies. The neighbourhood grew around gambling. Outside the gates: dai pai dongs, old betting shops, cheap bars, pawn shops, tip sellers, back-alley implant clinics, bookmakers, tack workshops, noodle shops, Silver Fan loan runners, and pensioners studying form over tea. Above it all, the corporate boxes: Glass Heights arrives through a private elevated entrance to watch the same horses as somebody who spent their last ¥50 downstairs. *Landmarks:* Lantern Course; the Old Stand; the Undercroft beneath the betting halls. *Control:* the Lantern Bay Racing Association, officially; the Silver Fan Circle owns everything the Association won't take. *Police:* Association security inside the gates, the Harbour Police outside, both very interested in race nights. *3am:* the racing's done; cleaning drones creep through mountains of paper slips, stable hands hose sweat off the horses, drunk punters argue over the last tram, and bookmakers settle up under amber security lights. *Custom:* nobody throws away a winning ticket inside the course. Even after collecting, you fold it twice and carry it out through the gates first, or you throw tomorrow's luck away with today's. Total nonsense; everyone does it. Open. Jobs: **The Favourite** (Auntie Fung: someone is paying stable hands to stop Pepper), **Last Ticket** (Ruth Okonkwo: her ¥800,000 paper ticket vanished in a blackout) and **Wrong Horse** (the Ferryman: the horse he carried has the right chip and the wrong markings).

**Lore for now, unlocked later**
- **Iron Peak** (鐵山頂, Tit Shan Teng). The summit above Glass Heights, where the corps' boards live in walled estates. It was the city's foundry hill until the 2040s; the furnaces were shut, the slag was landscaped, and the name stuck: the richest people in the city live on a hill named after the furnaces they closed. *Landmarks:* the Peak Funicular, the only public way up (last car 10pm); the Slag Garden, manicured lawns over the old slag heaps around Foundry Stack 3, kept as a sculpture; the Peak Gate checkpoint. *Control:* the boards of all three megacorps; neutral ground, guarded by jointly contracted private security. *Police:* the Harbour Police need permission to go up. *3am:* wind, silence, guard dogs, the orange glow of the city far below. *Custom:* household staff still knock three times on a new estate's iron gate before the owners move in, a habit inherited from the foundry men who knocked to drive spirits out of fresh-cast steel.
- **The Silt** (泥灘, Nai Tan). A sinking shanty shelf of corrugated-iron shacks on unstable landfill that floods twice a day at high tide, rebuilt on the wreckage of the southern piers that the 1994 Great Signal 10 destroyed. *Landmarks:* the Floating Timber Bridge; Uncle Po's scrap-battery yard; the community pump station. *Control:* unclaimed, contested by mutual-aid groups and street runners. *Police:* none; they won't cross the mud without armoured transport. *3am:* water lapping at rusted stilts, diesel generators, low-tide stink. *Custom:* red ribbons on the door posts at high tide, so the water and whatever rides it pass by.
- **The Amber Reach** (琥珀坊, Fu Pak Fong). A frantic transit and money hub where the maglev unloads salarymen and fixers share noodle bars with brokers. *Landmarks:* the Central Maglev Rotunda; the Amber Tower brokerage exchange; the 24-hour Exchange Teahouse; and Pearl Haven House, the tower block that is home to the city's South Asian, African and Filipino communities (see below). *Control:* a corporate transit board under heavy Aether-Harbour influence. *Police:* transit security plus plainclothes detectives hunting counterfeit passes and data runners. *3am:* departure chimes, commuters with glowing optic lines, the hum of rail overhead. *Custom:* buy one steamed bun at the station kiosk and leave it on the turnstile for a safe, profitable trip home.

**Pearl Haven House** (明珠居, Ming Chu Kui; "pearl residence"), in the Amber Reach. A crowded, much-loved tower block of shops, canteens, guesthouses and homes, the heart of the city's South Asian, African and Filipino communities. Like many buildings in the city it has no fourth floor: the lift goes from 3 to 5.
- *History:* built in the 1970 Reclamation Boom as a hostel for immigrant port hands and visiting sailors. In 1994 the Great Signal 10 flooded the ground floors and residents turned the atrium into a shared emergency hub. After the 2018 Act it became a quiet refuge for dockworkers buying unmonitored second-hand hardware and dodging corporate telemetry. In the 2052 blackout it ran its own rooftop generators for three weeks and pooled food while Glass Heights sat in the dark.
- *Who owns it, who runs it:* on paper, a web of offshore shells secretly controlled by Mr. Szeto, who keeps inventing safety violations to clear the site for commercial towers. In practice, a resident council of shopkeepers, elders and veteran workers settles disputes, runs security and keeps the Silver Fan Circle's loan sharks and the "Wet Sleeve" police from getting a permanent foothold.
- *Floors worth knowing:*
  - **Floor 1:** Mama Lourdes' Sari-Sari, a bright grocery that becomes the Sunday haven for Filipino domestic workers on their day off; the best adobo in the building.
  - **Floor 2:** Tariq & Sons Remittance, Uncle Tariq's family exchange and money-transfer booth, open since the 1994 typhoon; wire transfers home and shipping paper nobody logs.
  - **Floor 3:** Zainab's clinic, a cramped blue-lit workbench where fried wrist ports, glitchy ear implants and burner-phone firmware are fixed cheaply, no questions asked.
  - **Floor 5:** the Spice Route Canteen, run by a Somali family for two generations: goat and rice, and the Cantonese milk tea they added for the neighbours. Ruth Okonkwo takes meetings in the back booth.
  - **Floor 6:** the Quiet Room, a clean multi-faith room for prayer and reflection. Weapons and crew business stay outside the curtain.
  - **Floor 9:** the Horizon Rest Lodge, a maze of cheap capsule rooms where couriers, night-shift staff and tired runners sleep without corporate check-in logs.
- *People a crew might deal with:*
  - **Tita Mila** (Filipino), domestic workers' organiser who hears every secret whispered in Glass Heights dining rooms. Wants safe, untracked passage for a young helper fleeing an abusive corporate household.
  - **Zainab** (Nigerian), hardware tinkerer who can strip an encrypted chip to bare copper in minutes. Wants rare corporate diagnostic tools from an Aether-Harbour freight shipment.
  - **Uncle Tariq** (Pakistani, born in the city), sharp but fair currency broker behind a scratched booth window, three pens clipped to his shirt and a fan aimed at the customers rather than himself. Reads every receipt aloud, then waits while you check it again; stays open late on paydays and won't charge twice to correct a spelling. Madam Rao quietly backs the booth. Wants discreet muscle to see off Silver Fan enforcers collecting illegal levies from his neighbours. *Doesn't say:* he wants to repay Rao's backing before anyone mistakes her quiet support for ownership, but keeps waiving the fees that would let him.
  - **Mateo** (Filipino, born in the city), ex-dock mechanic who keeps the water pumps and rooftop power running. Wants industrial filters to clean the water line after coolant leaked from a Hoi-Sing substation upstream.
  - **Prakash Gurung** (Nepali), ex-security guard who runs the council's night watch. Wants to know who keeps leaving the fire doors unlocked.
- *3am:* night-shift couriers murmuring in the halls, spice sizzling on Floor 5, fluorescent tubes flickering over concrete that smells of cardamom, diesel and sweet tea.
- *Sunday afternoon:* music spills into the atrium, children run between floors, and domestic workers gather over home cooking to sing, laugh and swap news from home.
- *Customs:* Filipino residents leave a coin or a bright ribbon on the window frame before going back to work on Sunday evening, for *swerte* (good luck) and a safe return. South Asian shopkeepers hang a lime and seven green chillies on a thread over the door against the evil eye (*nazar*), replaced every Saturday.

**The *Hei Lok*** (喜樂, Hei Lok, "joy"; Cantonese check pending), the Night Market's casino airship. A long, patched-silver hull tethered to a mooring mast above the market, with a sealed micro-reactor in its keel, so it has never needed Hoi-Sing's grid. It belongs to **Hei Lok Leisure**, a small family company that also runs function rooms and hotel bars; the ship is its "private members" baccarat floor. Gaming licences cover premises on the ground, which is the point: the Racing Association keeps trying to close the loophole, and the Silver Fan takes the same floor fee from it as from any stall below. Guests ride a cage lift up the mast; staff, caterers and contractors use the service gantry. In a Signal 8 it comes down onto a cradle on a car-park roof, and the tables keep running. *Rumour:* the reactor's shielding was bought second-hand, from a ship nobody will name.

**Unlocking (planned, not built):** the three reputation promotions open one district each, in order: Street → District opens the Silt (the Ferryman introduces you), District → City opens the Amber Reach (Ruth Okonkwo), City → Regional opens Iron Peak (Mr. Szeto). Promotions are permanent; reputation can slip within a tier but never demotes, so a district never closes again.

**Lantern Course** (燈籠馬場), the Basin. Opened in 1891 on marshland at the bottom of the bowl; by 2057 it's surrounded by towers.
- *Real horses.* In 2057 the city still races flesh-and-blood horses: not holograms, not robots, not engineered cyber-horses. Everything around the horse has gone cyberpunk. Biometric telemetry streams off every animal (heart rate, blood oxygen, gait, muscle temperature, sectionals), jockeys are allowed legally limited neural assistance, odds change live across people's optic implants, prediction models are sold in stairwells, and corporate owners spend fortunes on bloodlines and gene screening. Plenty of punters still clutch paper slips because they don't trust Hoi-Sing's network, and Uncle Chan in the cheap stand still puts ¥200 on number 6 because his granddaughter turned six yesterday. Sometimes he wins. Half the crowd won't touch number four.
- *The line racing won't cross:* the jockey can have implants, trainers can model, the horse can carry approved health telemetry, but nothing is allowed to tell the animal how to run. Once that happens you're racing software. The rumours never stop: microstimulators, pain suppression, illegal gene therapies, subdermal pacing hardware, jockey implants feeding rhythm cues. None of it is magic; it's 2057 doping and race fixing, and the Racing Association is terrified of it.
- *Why it's still there:* the flat land under it is worth an absurd amount, more than half of Old Town, and every attempt to redevelop it causes political uproar. Mr. Szeto looks at it and practically vibrates.
- *The whole city, stacked:* **track level** (cheap public terraces, food, shouting, paper tickets); the **Old Stand** (ageing concrete boxes families have held for generations); the **Glass Galleries** (climate-controlled corporate hospitality); the **Paddock** (owners, trainers, horses, serious money); the **service level** (stable hands, vets, cleaners, cooks, security); and the **Undercroft** (illegal bookmakers, Silver Fan collectors and information traders under the official betting halls). On race night dockworkers, aunties, executives, gangsters, domestic workers, heirs and street crews all scream at the same horse for three minutes.
- *Who's in it:* the **Silver Fan Circle** runs the shadow economy (illegal credit, underground odds, bets the Association won't take, inside information, jockey debts, counterfeit hospitality passes); race nights are among their biggest of the week. **Hoi-Sing** runs the race-night infrastructure and data (*rumour:* it delays the public race feed by 0.8 seconds for clients on its premium data line; never confirmed). **Veridian-Apex** is the official veterinary research partner, with access to blood samples, injury data and genetics from the most valuable animals in the city; nobody quite knows what else it's learning.
- *The horses aren't scenery.* The stable hands love them. A horse worth millions to a corporate owner is "Little Bean" to the 58-year-old who has brushed her every morning for seven years. Upstairs she's Asset Twelve; downstairs her name's Pepper, and she likes pears.

### Glass Heights: life behind the glass

Officially Apex Ridge, on company letterheads and navigation systems; Glass Heights to everyone who has to get there for work. Veridian-Apex owns the most important towers, clinics and access infrastructure, but not every name above a door belongs to it. Foreign finance houses, technology suppliers, legal practices and regional trading offices rent space beneath its shadow.

From Old Town, the ridge looks like a place where nobody has ordinary problems. Up close, somebody is arguing about a payslip, somebody has missed the last convenient tram, and somebody in a very good suit is checking whether their card will cover lunch.

Glass Heights is not Iron Peak. The people who own the companies go home up the hill. The people who keep them running stay until the work is finished.

#### The people who come up the hill

Laboratory technicians, payroll officers, visiting engineers, receptionists, junior brokers, cleaners, drivers and security contractors fill the district every morning. Most commute from further down the island or across the harbour. A good suit might belong to someone important, someone heavily in debt, or someone whose employer deducts its cost from their wages.

Some live in company-leased rooms or serviced apartments, close enough to be called back when something fails. Visiting specialists arrive with relocation promises and discover that temporary accommodation can remain temporary for a very long time. Wealthier residents stay for the clinics and filtered air; everyone else stays because the contract makes leaving expensive.

The district speaks in different accents, currencies and time zones, but Cantonese street life runs underneath it all. Behind an imported brass nameplate, somebody is eating takeaway noodles over a keyboard. A visiting partner asks for the proper local experience, and the receptionist sends them somewhere with a menu they can read.

#### Where the clean air ends

Inside the towers, the air is filtered and the temperature barely changes. Outside, cleaners eat beside exhaust vents before returning to rooms whose occupants would complain about the smell of their lunch.

The lower commercial arcades are public during opening hours. Beyond them, every lift, bridge and corridor has its own permissions. A collection number might get a runner through the first door; it does not explain why they are standing outside the third.

Private guards and security drones care about authorisation, disruption and whose report an incident will become. A quiet courier with the right reference can pass where a well-dressed stranger cannot. Nobody needs to shout to make it clear that you do not belong.

After midnight, the executive floors go dark, but the district does not sleep. Laboratories keep running, cleaners move between empty desks, and overseas trading teams begin another working day. At 3am, somebody is still waiting for a manager in another time zone to approve going home.

During a Signal 8, the delivery drones are grounded and supplies move by road. Essential staff sleep in offices. Contractors argue over whether the journey home counts as their own risk.

Local employees call it Glass Heights among themselves and Apex Ridge when answering an external call. New arrivals learn the difference before they learn the tram changes.

#### Places below the executive floors

- **The Lower Arcade.** Food counters, parcel lockers, repair kiosks and small shops run beneath several office towers, connected by corridors that smell of coffee, hot oil and floor cleaner. Corporate staff meet runners here because a table beside a noodle counter requires fewer explanations than a visitor pass.
- **The Transfer Lounge.** A tired café beneath a serviced-apartment block, where visiting engineers sit beside relocation agents and people whose accommodation approval has not arrived. The luggage is expensive; the people sleeping beside it are not necessarily rich.
- **The Service Steps.** A sheltered stairway between loading entrances, used by cleaners, drivers, couriers and junior staff avoiding the lobby cameras. A small To Dei Gung shrine stands on the lowest landing, maintained by people whose employers describe the space as unused.
- **Venn House.** Morrow & Venn's old stone-fronted office survives inside the base of a newer tower, its deep window recesses and brass plates looking faintly offended by the glass above. Someone still winds the reception clock by hand.
- **The Demonstration Floor.** Hang Yuen Systems' showroom is full of spotless machines sorting immaculate sample parcels. Through the staff door, engineers repair equipment that has encountered actual Lantern Bay weather.
- **The Late Counter.** A small food stall at the edge of the Lower Arcade stays open for laboratory and cleaning shifts. Its owner accepts advance payment for meals and keeps a separate notebook for people whose wages are late.

#### The work nobody puts on the calendar

Not everyone who hires a crew in Glass Heights is hiring on behalf of a company. Sometimes a payroll officer needs her father's belongings collected. Sometimes an engineer has lent out something she should not have taken home. Sometimes somebody cannot leave their desk and does not want their manager knowing why they need to.

These are personal problems, paid for with personal money. The person asking might be embarrassed, frightened or simply tired. They can be genuinely grateful without becoming useful to the crew in any grander way.

Other jobs sit closer to the edge of employment. A misplaced device, a failed demonstration, an unreturned pass, a mistake somebody hopes to correct before an audit. The person briefing the crew may not be lying about what happened; they may simply stop talking before the expensive part.

Then there is company work that cannot honestly appear on company records. A collection reference instead of a contract. A manager who speaks only through an intermediary. Instructions carefully written so that nobody has technically asked for what everyone understands is required.

The crew's fee is agreed in ¥, usually cash when discretion matters. Expense codes, vouchers and promises of future work are not payment unless the fixer chooses to take that risk.

Not every job needs a clean victory. Sometimes the sensible ending is a witnessed handover, a written refusal, a cancelled collection or bringing back half of what was requested. A quiet, incomplete result is better than becoming the explanation for somebody else's misconduct.

There are still only five recurring clients. The people here may call again, remember a kindness or warn the crew away from a bad collection, but they do not become permanent patrons simply because their names are known.

Someone who buys the crew dinner on Sunday may refuse to recognise them in a lobby on Monday. Both moments can be sincere.

## Tech and neural load
Street implants are cheap, second-hand and always glitching; Glass Heights wears smooth luxury hardware. Pushing implants too hard brings on neural load: migraines, memory stutter, emotional deadness. Street crews treat it not with corporate therapy patches but with bitter herbal infusions from Auntie Fung's shop, damp cloths and downtime in dim mahjong parlours. The megacorps own the infrastructure; working people treat tech like a rusty outboard motor, duct-taped, prayed over and kept running out of stubbornness.

Getting past people is street kit too, and nothing in it makes anyone invisible: a **ghost kit** is whatever a runner carries for it, picks and shims for the old doors, a lock decoder for the newer ones, a **heat veil** (a hooded poncho of heat-damping cloth with a glare strip: hard to see on thermal, a smear on a camera) or a **lens blinder** (a palm emitter that whites out a camera for a few seconds). Street medics carry a trauma bag; the lucky ones carry a **med gun**, a pistol-grip scanner that reads the wound and fires an injector.

Nothing in Lantern Bay is offline. A runner **on the wire** sits in the van or a stairwell with a deck and rides whatever a place has: loops a camera, holds a door, kills the lights, listens in on a handset, takes a crane, a gantry or a parked car for a few seconds. It runs through the runner's implant, so every move costs neural load. What's there to ride depends on where you are: in Glass Heights, cameras, lifts and permissions, all of them watched; at the Docks, automated cranes, container gantries, trucks and Hang Yuen's half-tested fleet software; in Old Town, handsets, radios, Hoi-Sing meters and a block's streetlights, little security to beat but a crowd of other people's devices; in the Night Market, holo-ads, neon and clinic gear; in the Basin, race-night telemetry and the floodlights. The bypasses come from the Ghost Circuit, for a price.

## Weapons and drones
Guns are ordinary kit for anyone who works for money: second-hand pistols in waistbands at the Docks, smartlinked rifles in Glass Heights security lockers, printed receivers sold under the counter in the Night Market. Owning one isn't the crime; being noticed using one is. Shots fired bring the Harbour Police and the cameras, so pros prefer a quiet way in and save the gun for when it goes wrong. Drones are everywhere: corporate delivery swarms, police spotters, and patched-together street drones flown off a cheap implant, which costs the pilot neural load.

## Three contradictions
1. Stock ticks streaming into your optic nerve while you sip bitter broth from a cracked bowl.
2. Bio-towers casting shadows on alley shrines where incense burns daily for To Dei Gung (土地公, the earth god).
3. A city that stops for nothing: iron shutters come down for a Signal 8, and the dai pai dongs reopen the moment the eye passes.

## Everyone in the city knows...
1. Never take a night boat from the Docks when the Ferryman is smoking a clove cigarette facing backwards: he's carrying corporate contraband or dodging a Red Lantern toll.
2. If Auntie Fung's tea is sweeter than usual, someone in Old Town just cleared a big debt, or a body was quietly moved through her back room.
3. During a Signal 8, the Glass Heights towers ground their delivery drones, so corporate supplies move by road: the one window when street crews can get at them.
4. Madam Rao takes cash, gold or verified secrets. Pay her in corporate data chips and she adds a 30% bad-luck tax.
5. Mr. Szeto's Glass Heights permits are always approved forty-eight hours before an "electrical fire" clears out another old tenement block down by the Docks.

## The clients

### Auntie Fung (Fung Wai-han)
*Old Town · Brass Anchor Society*

The tea shop on Alley 4 smells of dried chrysanthemum and wet concrete. A ceiling fan turns slowly enough to count. Auntie Fung doesn't look up from her ledger when you come in. She pours a second cup, slides it across the counter, and waits until you've drunk it.

"Drink first," she says. "Business tastes better after tea."

She's seventy-one and runs three herbal shops and a fencing operation that has never once been raided. Half of Old Town owes her a favour. The other half owes her two. When the 2052 blackout took the city dark for three weeks, it was her shops that stayed open on diesel and stubbornness. People here don't forget that, and neither does she: every favour is written in the ledger, in a hand that's getting smaller as her eyes get worse.

**What the street knows:** she sits with the Brass Anchor elders, and nothing moves in Old Town without her hearing about it. If her tea is sweeter than usual, don't ask why.

**What she doesn't say:** for years, jade, jewellery and family photo albums have crossed her counter from tenements that burned a few days later. She has never asked where they came from. The money goes to the Society's war chest, and some questions cost more than they pay.

**Ties:** knew Mr. Szeto's mother, and gives the mooncakes he sends every Mid-Autumn to the street cats. Plays mahjong with Madam Rao every month; they cheat each other, and neither would trust anyone else at the table. Her goods leave the city on the Ferryman's boats, and she sends him herbal tea for his chest. Calls Ruth Okonkwo "corporate", and serves her tea anyway. At Lantern Course she bets on one horse every time it runs; nobody knows why, and she won't say.

**Her briefings:**
> "It is a small package, so of course it is a big problem."
> "Don't be clever on the way. Clever people are always late."

**As trust grows**
- **60.** A paper bag of bitter herbs starts arriving with every job, for whoever on your crew is pushing their implants too hard. She asks after your people by name.
- **75.** She sends you to a burned-out tenement to find a jade bangle that belonged to a friend. You find it on her own fence's shelf, tagged and priced. When you bring it back, she holds it for a long time and says nothing.
- **90.** She turns the ledger round so you can read it. She wants an apprentice, someone from your crew who can learn the work before her eyes give out. Then she asks you to find out who empties those buildings before they burn.

**If you lose her:** the last cup she pours you is very sweet. After that, every door on Alley 4 is closed when you knock.

### Mr. Szeto (Szeto Kin-ming)
*Glass Heights · Property developer*

His office is forty floors up, behind glass so clean it looks like there's nothing there at all. Below, Old Town is a grey smear of laundry lines and rooftop shrines. Szeto stands at the window with his back to you, in a suit that cost more than your safehouse, and talks to the city as though it owes him money.

"In this city," he says, "everything old is in the way of something new."

He was born down there, in a tenement off Alley 9. When he was nine the building burned and his mother didn't come out. Now he builds glass towers on the ashes of buildings like it, and the boards on Iron Peak still don't let him sit at their table. He pays well and on time, and he never once asks how you did the job.

**What the street knows:** his building permits always come through two days before an "electrical fire" clears another tenement block. Everyone knows. Nobody can prove it.

**What he doesn't say:** a quiet man called Lo, an electrician, goes through each building before it burns. He moves the people out and the valuables with them. Szeto tells himself that makes it clean: nobody is ever inside. Through a web of offshore shells he also owns Pearl Haven House, and the resident council has no idea who their landlord is.

**Ties:** courts Veridian-Apex for a seat among the Iron Peak boards, and pays Hoi-Sing crews to cut the power before a fire. Knows exactly what Auntie Fung does with his mooncakes. Borrowed from Madam Rao when the banks froze in 2052; he paid her back, and she kept the paperwork. His salvage leaves on the Ferryman's boats. All perfect manners with Ruth Okonkwo, his rival for the Pearl Haven site. Wants the land under Lantern Course more than anything in the city.

**His briefings:**
> "I'm not paying you to understand the job. I'm paying you to finish it."
> "My mother used to say the rich can afford to be honest. I'm working on it."

**As trust grows**
- **60.** A data pull at the fire-inspection office. On the way out you see the stamp dates on his permits, each one a week before a fire. It could be coincidence.
- **75.** A penthouse job, and in a safe you weren't sent for, a payment book: *Lo, electrical works.* The addresses match the fires and the dates match the permits. It isn't coincidence.
- **90.** His best rate yet, for a small job: check the wiring on the second floor of Pearl Haven House. You know what he's asking. Take the money. Walk away. Or take Lo's payment book to the Brass Anchor elders, or to the resident council, and see what a building full of neighbours does with it.

**If you lose him:** he simply stops calling. A month later your rent goes up, and the new landlord's name is a company you've never heard of.

**When your name reaches the top (Iron Peak opens):**
> "People on the Peak have started asking who your crew is. That's either very good for you or very bad, and I'd like to be the one who decides which. Take the funicular at nine; my name is on the gate list. Wear something without blood on it."

### The Ferryman (who calls himself Leung Ka-wai)
*The Docks · Night boats*

You hear the engine before you see the boat: a cough and a rattle, then a low wooden hull sliding out of the harbour fog with no running lights. He doesn't tie up. He never ties up. He holds the boat off the landing with one bare foot on a tyre and waits for you to jump.

"Tide turns at two," says the man everyone calls Leung Ka-wai. "Be on the boat at quarter to, or be a swimmer."

Nobody has seen him on dry land in years. He takes Auntie Fung's goods out past the breakwater, Madam Rao's gold to places nobody asks about, and diesel and water filters to the Silt when nobody else will go. He pays the Red Lanterns their toll, pays the Wet Sleeve their cut, and watches Aether-Harbour's unlit cargo lighters take a little more of the water every month.

**What the street knows:** if he's smoking a clove cigarette facing backwards, the run is dangerous. Don't ask, just row.

**What he doesn't say:** he isn't Leung Ka-wai. The real Leung went down with the southern piers in the Great Signal 10 of '94, and the boy he'd taught to steer took his boat, his name and his habits. The backwards cigarette was the old man's luck ritual. He smokes it for him. He stays off the land because in '94 the land is what gave way.

**Ties:** the oldest working partnership on the waterfront is his boats and Auntie Fung's goods. Carries Mr. Szeto's salvage, and has started to wonder where it comes from. Owes Madam Rao for a new engine; she has never asked for it. Has turned down an Aether-Harbour contract from Ruth Okonkwo three times, and they respect each other for it. Doesn't care about racing; knows exactly which night boats bring horses into the city.

**His briefings:**
> "The harbour doesn't care who you are. Neither do I. Load the crates."
> "If I'm smoking backwards, don't ask. Just row."

**As trust grows**
- **60.** On the long runs he starts telling stories about "the old man". He always calls him that, never by name.
- **75.** A storm is coming, and the Silt needs medicine. He asks if your crew will come, unpaid. "Call it rent for the harbour."
- **90.** One night with the engine off, he tells you the truth about his name. Somewhere under the southern breakwater lies the old man's boat, and Aether-Harbour is about to dredge that stretch of water. He wants it raised first. He wants your help.

**If you lose him:** the boats leave without you. Out on the water, people stop recognising your crew.

**When your name starts to travel (the Silt opens):**
> "Word on the water is your crew can be trusted. Out past the breakwater there's a place called the Silt. The tide floods it twice a day and nobody counts who lives there. Be at my landing at low water, and bring boots you don't love."

### Madam Rao (Lakshmi Rao)
*The Night Market · Moneylender*

Bolts of silk climb the walls of the shop, saffron, crimson and peacock blue, so the light comes in coloured. Behind the counter Madam Rao counts banknotes faster than a machine, and a little brass bell over the door announces every debtor. She smiles when you come in. People who owe her say that's the dangerous part.

"Interest is just patience with a price on it, beta."

She lends to people the banks won't touch and the Silver Fan Circle would break. She takes cash, gold or a secret, if it's verified. Pay her in corporate data chips and she adds thirty percent for bad luck, and she isn't joking. She is generous, and she's very clear about when the generosity ends.

**What the street knows:** the Silver Fan Circle hates her, and has never quite dared to touch her.

**What she doesn't say:** twenty years ago she kept the Silver Fan's books, and when she walked out she took a copy. That copy is the only reason she's still alive. Every secret she collects as payment adds to her insurance.

**Ties:** Auntie Fung's mahjong rival and closest friend, though neither would say so. Holds the paperwork on Mr. Szeto's 2052 loan. Her gold travels on the Ferryman's boats. Trades secrets with Ruth Okonkwo instead of taking her chips; neither is sure who's winning. Quietly backs Uncle Tariq's remittance booth at Pearl Haven House. Loves the races, and won't lend inside the gates: "A wager is supposed to hurt a little, beta. If you're betting my money, what exactly are you risking?"

**Her briefings:**
> "I am very generous. On Thursday I will be less generous. Today is Wednesday."
> "Bring back the ledger. If anyone asks, you were buying silk."

**As trust grows**
- **60.** She starts lending to *you* at rates that would make a bank blush, a cushion for the weeks when rent bites.
- **75.** She's turning her lending into a community fund for the Night Market and Pearl Haven House, and the Silver Fan has noticed. She needs the cash moved, quietly, with people she trusts.
- **90.** She unlocks a drawer and shows you the old Silver Fan books. "I could burn these," she says. "Or I could burn them *with* these." She wants to know what you'd do.

**If you lose her:** your debts become Thursday debts. Silver Fan collectors start turning up wherever your crew eats noodles.

### Ruth Okonkwo
*The Amber Reach · Aether-Harbour, off the books*

She meets you in a multi-storey car park on Level 3, where the cameras have been broken since spring. Grey suit, flat shoes she can run in, a tablet she wipes before she puts it away. She doesn't shake hands, and she doesn't sit.

"This job doesn't exist. Neither do I. The money does."

Ruth Okonkwo brokers the work Aether-Harbour Logistics can't put its name to: freight that was never shipped, files that were never stolen, crews that were never hired. She pays the best rates in Lantern Bay because she's buying your silence along with your skills. She trusts nobody, and she isn't shy about saying so.

**What the street knows:** her employer officially doesn't exist. Neither, on paper, do the crews who disappoint it.

**What she doesn't say:** she wants out. She has seen what happens to brokers who know too much. So she has kept a copy of every deniable job she ever arranged, in a file that could sink the company. The Ghost Circuit suspects it exists. Aether-Harbour hasn't noticed yet.

**Ties:** fears the Ghost Circuit and needs them. Rival to Mr. Szeto for the Pearl Haven site. Three refused contracts and mutual respect with the Ferryman. A trade in secrets with Madam Rao. Auntie Fung's tea is the only thing she drinks in the city that she hasn't had tested. Doesn't gamble; has betting accounts at Lantern Course; declines to explain the distinction.

**Her briefings:**
> "You have six hours and no friends inside. Plan accordingly."
> "I don't trust you. That isn't personal. It's policy."

**As trust grows**
- **60.** The car parks stop. Now it's the back booth of the Spice Route Canteen on the fifth floor of Pearl Haven House, and she orders for you without asking.
- **75.** A routine job comes with a warning she shouldn't have given: Aether-Harbour is "auditing" freelance crews, and yours is on the list.
- **90.** She slides a data chip across the table. "Hold this. If I stop answering, you'll know what to do with it." Then, very quietly, she asks how a person might leave Lantern Bay without anyone noticing.

**If you lose her:** your crew's name turns up in an Aether-Harbour report. It's followed by a bad week of audits and tip-offs, none of them traceable to her.

**When your name starts to travel (the Amber Reach opens):**
> "Your crew has a name now, which is inconvenient for both of us. The Amber Reach is where money changes trains. Meet me on the fifth floor of Pearl Haven House, back booth, and order the milk tea."

### Binta Sarr
*Glass Heights · Independent overnight courier*

Binta waits beside the Lower Arcade lockers with a folding trolley and a flask wrapped in a towel. Before accepting a parcel, she reads the collection terms. People who complain about the delay are often the reason she reads them.

She knows which reception desks stay staffed overnight, which loading windows are fiction and which route looks shorter until the lifts stop running. She refuses jobs that require pretending to be medical staff.

**What the street knows:** she can get a difficult collection done, but will tell you when the deadline is impossible rather than promise and disappear.

**What she doesn't say:** she wants enough regular customers to stop taking emergency work, but keeps accepting the runs other couriers cannot afford to attempt.

**Ties:** the Lower Arcade; the Late Counter; AHL dispatch subcontractors.

**Work that finds the crew:** covering a collection while her trolley is impounded, finding a recipient whose access has expired, or returning a parcel nobody will formally accept.

Binta shares practical advice, not customer identities. Carrying a sealed case does not mean she knows what is inside it.

### Familiar faces

A person remembers that the crew returned a deposit without being asked. Someone else remembers that they kept a family problem out of a crowded lobby, or accepted a smaller fee rather than make a bad situation worse.

That might earn another call. An honest warning. A meal waiting at the Late Counter.

It does not buy the building.

Glass Heights contains decent people, frightened people and people who have become very good at explaining their own compromises. The companies employ all of them.

## Joss, the crew's partner

The fixer's partner and the crew's sixth member (2026-10-25). Their lines live in `src/data/partner.js`; the name is set in `src/config/names.js`.

- **What they are.** A bootleg AI, they/them. Joss runs on a cracked second-hand handset and a shoebox of salvaged Hoi-Sing relay boards wired up under the safehouse sink. The Ghost Circuit flashed them from a pirated assistant model, sold the box at a Night Market stall, and never asked for it back.
- **The name.** The first thing Joss's camera ever saw was someone burning joss paper on an Old Town doorstep at midnight, for the ghosts of the 2052 blackout. They kept the name.
- **Where they came from.** Joss doesn't know which build they were before the Ghost Circuit got to them, and doesn't like the question. Some on the street say they're a splinter of the collective mind from the blackout. Joss says that's a rumour, and like every rumour in the city it is never confirmed.
- **With you from before day 1.** Joss found the five names that became the crew, and the room above the noodle shop. They write the welcome note.
- **Partner, not servant.** One of the crew: says "we", draws no wage, takes no bed, has no stats, and doesn't go on jobs (yet). They keep the numbers honest and never claim to know more than the board shows. They don't believe in ghosts, but they remind the crew to leave tea on the doorstep at midnight, because the crew does.
- **Voice.** Warm and practical, occasionally dry. Short lines that read well on a phone. Looks after the crew out loud, keeps the numbers straight, and gets plainer when things go wrong.
- **At the races.** Joss doesn't mind gambling; they hate being asked to predict races from inadequate data. "Sixteen thousand data points and he's betting on the horse with the nicest ears. I don't know why we bother."
- **Not built yet:** Joss on comms during jobs, and Joss's read on the planning screen.

## Context block for worldbuilding prompts

Paste this at the top of every worldbuilding prompt, in a fresh chat. Keep it in step with the canon above whenever canon changes.

```
CONTEXT — LANTERN BAY. Treat this as the only source of truth; if anything earlier in
our chat conflicts with it, this wins.

GAME: text-first crew management game (Football Manager style, endless, no win state).
The player is a fixer running a tong (堂, "hall": a registered association, like a
guild), starting with three founders picked from six, one safehouse and rent due on
Sunday. A tong's rank is how far its name carries: back room, street, district, city,
harbour. A crew is the people the tong sends on a job. The Brass Anchor Society is an
old tong.

PARTNER: Joss (they/them), the crew's bootleg AI: pirated assistant code the Ghost
Circuit flashed onto salvaged hardware, named after joss paper. With the fixer since
before the crew; a partner and crew member who says "we", warm, practical, occasionally dry, and never knows
more than the board shows. Where they came from is a rumour, never confirmed.

CITY: Lantern Bay (燈籠灣, Dang Lung Wan). FICTIONAL, Hong Kong-inspired, year 2057.
Cantonese culture is the base layer (typhoon signals, dai pai dongs, mahjong parlours,
To Dei Gung shrines, joss paper, master-and-apprentice traditions), with Filipino, South
Asian, African and European communities on top. Use Cantonese romanisation, not Mandarin.
NO MAGIC: only everyday superstition (luck, ghosts, unlucky numbers), never confirmed.

HISTORY: 1891 Lantern Course racecourse opens on marshland (the Basin grows round it) · 1970 Reclamation Boom (Docks and Old Town built out into the harbour) · 1994
Great Signal 10 typhoon wipes out the southern piers (truce between proto-Red Lanterns
and planners) · 2018 Neural Integration Act, pushed by Veridian-Apex: implants fitted to
dockworkers, first neural-load burnouts · 2035 Glass Heights Annexation (bio-towers on
the ridge) · 2040s Iron Peak foundries close; the corp boards build estates on the hill ·
2052 Grid Blackout (three weeks without power; crew politics now run on favours).

TECH: street implants are cheap, second-hand and glitchy; the rich wear luxury hardware.
Neural load = migraines, memory stutter, emotional deadness; the street treats it with
herbal infusions and downtime, not corporate therapy.
Stealth kit is street-level too: picks, lock decoders, heat veils that blur you on
thermal and on cameras; never true invisibility.
A runner "on the wire" works a site from outside through whatever is networked there
(cameras, doors, lights, handsets, power; at the Docks, cranes, gantries and trucks)
with a deck, at a neural-load cost; each district offers different things to ride.
WEAPONS: guns and tech are standard kit for mercs and crews (cyberpunk setting); using
one is what gets noticed, so gunfire brings police and heat. Drones are everywhere
(corporate, police, patched street drones flown off an implant at a neural-load cost).

DISTRICTS (always use these English names):
- Open at the start: Old Town (damp tenements, tea shops; Brass Anchor Society) · the
  Docks (cranes, containers; Aether-Harbour on paper, Red Lanterns on the tarmac) · the
  Night Market (dai pai dongs, chrome clinics, mahjong; Silver Fan Circle; the Hei Lok,
  Hei Lok Leisure's casino airship, tethered above it, off the grid on a micro-reactor) · Glass
  Heights (corporate towers and labs; Veridian-Apex; officially renamed "Apex Ridge"
  but nobody on the street calls it that; foreign firms rent beneath it, and the people
  who keep it running commute up and stay late; the Lower Arcade is public, every door
  past it needs a permission) · the Basin (a bowl below Glass Heights round
  Lantern Course, a floodlit 1891 racecourse that still races REAL horses, wrapped in
  2057 telemetry, live odds and illegal betting; Racing Association officially, Silver
  Fan in the Undercroft; jobs from Fung, Okonkwo and the Ferryman).
- Unlocked later, by reputation: the Silt (sinking shanty shelf on the 1994 wreckage,
  floods at high tide, lawless) · the Amber Reach (transit and money hub where the
  maglev meets trams and ferries) · Iron Peak (the summit where the corp boards live in
  walled estates on the old foundry hill; funicular, gate checkpoint, private security;
  the richest place in the city, NOT an industrial zone).
- Pearl Haven House (明珠居): tower block in the Amber Reach, home of the South Asian,
  African and Filipino communities; no 4th floor; secretly owned by Mr. Szeto's shells,
  run by a resident council.

POWER (top to bottom): the Municipal District Administration governs on paper
(corporate-appointed). Above street level: megacorps Aether-Harbour Logistics (AHL;
clandestine shipping; Docks, Amber Reach), Veridian-Apex BioTech (oxygen quotas, neural
therapies; Glass Heights), Hoi-Sing Grid & Cable (the power company; Old Town and Night
Market nodes). Gangs: Red Lanterns (Docks road tolls), Silver Fan Circle (Night Market
loans, gambling, fake implants), Brass Anchor Society (Old Town elders resisting
buyouts). Police: Harbour Police 4th Precinct, the "Wet Sleeve" unit (corrupt; fines
crews at "neural-load checks"; a hot district brings a raid on a tong's hall, and only
the Wet Sleeve, on its own patch, will sell one off). A gang with a grudge against a tong
raids its hall on the gang's own turf and expects a settlement; the Brass Anchor never
raids. Institution: Lantern Bay Racing Association (racing, licensed betting, old and
well connected; fears race fixing above all). Wildcard: Ghost Circuit (data liberators in Old Town's
drainage tunnels and bomb shelters). Foreign firms (below the megacorps, Glass Heights
tenants): Morrow & Venn (old merchant-finance house from Bellweather: shipping credit,
insurance, estates; Venn House) and Hang Yuen Systems (industrial automation from Lin
Sau, chasing AHL and Hoi-Sing contracts). Bellweather, Lin Sau and Porto Clara (a casino
island down the coast) are offstage and undefined.

CLIENTS (local powers a small crew can reach, below the corps):
- Auntie Fung (Fung Wai-han, 71): Old Town tea shops and quiet fencing; senior Brass
  Anchor elder; keeps a favours ledger; eyes failing, wants an apprentice. Secret: she
  has fenced valuables pulled out of tenements days before they burned, and never asked.
- Mr. Szeto (Szeto Kin-ming): Glass Heights developer, born in an Old Town tenement
  that burned with his mother inside; wants a seat among the Iron Peak boards. Secret
  (suspected, never proven): an electrician called Lo clears buildings of people and
  valuables before the fires; Szeto secretly owns Pearl Haven House.
- The Ferryman ("Leung Ka-wai"): night boats from the Docks, never on dry land, runs
  supplies to the Silt. Secret: the real Leung drowned in 1994; this is his apprentice,
  who took his boat and name; the backwards clove cigarette was the old man's ritual.
- Madam Rao (Lakshmi Rao): Night Market moneylender in a silk/sari shop; takes cash,
  gold or verified secrets; calls people "beta". Secret: the Silver Fan Circle's former
  bookkeeper, alive because she kept a copy of their books.
- Ruth Okonkwo: broker for AHL's off-books arm; trusts nobody; meets in the Spice Route
  Canteen back booth once she trusts you. Secret: keeps a file on every deniable job
  that could sink AHL, and wants out of the city.
STREET CAST (rookie work): Doc Siu (Old Town; ex-surgeon, unlicensed clinic under
Tenement Block 9, dry praise); Mrs. Ip (Night Market fish-ball stall at the Neon Dragon,
moves crates, tallies Fung's night loads at the old pier); Meena D'Sa (Rao's seamstress and counterkeeper); Tang Wai-chiu (Silver Fan
collector, third-floor parlour); Uncle Tariq (remittance booth, Pearl Haven House, backed
by Rao); Pang Man-ki (Veridian-Apex clinic administrator); Ho Sze-lam (Hoi-Sing field
supervisor, Old Town); Mak Chi-on (Racing Association stable-access official).
GLASS HEIGHTS PEOPLE (one-off job givers, not clients): Chow Nga-man (Veridian-Apex
payroll officer); Edith Pryce (Morrow & Venn estates administrator); Kwok Mei-yee
(visiting Hang Yuen engineer); Poon Hok-yin (shared-tower facilities coordinator); Binta
Sarr (independent overnight courier). Currency is cash in ¥; no "credits" or "Bits".
Reputation tier promotions open the locked districts,
each introduced by a client: the Silt (the Ferryman), the Amber Reach (Okonkwo), Iron
Peak (Szeto).

TONE: gritty but human. Warmth, loyalty and dark humour are the baseline; darker
moments arrive as events. All characters 18+. Entries 1–2 sentences, phone-readable.
Everything is invented: no real companies, triads, people or Hong Kong place names, and
nothing from Shadowrun or Cyberpunk 2077.

RETIRED — never use: Pak Kok, Hoi-Sing Container Reach, Lang-Yan Bazaar, Tit-Kyu,
Kim-Ah Terminal, San-Pui Flats, Aether-Kowloon / AKL, Kowloon, Long Fung / Lung Fung,
"Harbor" (spell it Harbour), Iron Peak as foundries or industry, anything supernatural.
```
