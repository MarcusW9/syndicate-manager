# New fight jobs: before and after (2026-11-15)

Five jobs drafted with a `/* FIGHT */` block were wired onto engine 2 (`FIGHT_JOBS`, `FIGHT_RECIPES`) and simmed with the
commands at the top of `slice3i-2026-11-15.md`. Four shipped; **The Wrong Fare was held back** (`drafts/jobs/thewrongfare.js`)
because its fight broke both declared bars. The "after" reports were run with it in, so they still show its rows.

Reports: `*-2026-11-15-before-newjobs` (main as it stood, sims unchanged), `*-2026-11-15-before-newjobs-sim` (main as it stood,
sims fixed, below), `*-2026-11-15-after-newjobs` (the new jobs, sims fixed). `sim-standard-force` printed the same table
before and after (it only plays Warehouse Lift and Data Scrub).

## The sims did not fight what the game fights (fixed)

`sim-gate-teams` and `sim-teams` built each fight without the job's `opposition` or `job` (so no standard force and no recipe).
The game passes both (`core/runfight.js` startFight, `core/squad.js` the planning read). Both sims now pass them. That is an
instrument change, so it was run on the old code too (`before-newjobs-sim`), to separate it from the new jobs.

**Warehouse Lift (police) was already over both bars in the real game** once the sim fights it with its police:

| Gate, best team | Old sim | Fixed sim, old code | Fixed sim, new jobs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| warehouse steady: clean win / left behind | 78% / 8% | 63% / 25% (over 15%) | 64% / 24% (over 15%) |
| warehouse committed: a death | 4.3% | 10.0% (over 8%) | 9.9% (over 8%) |
| data_scrub steady: clean win | 87% | 69% | 69% |

The new jobs do not move the old five (warehouse 63% to 64% is the campaign rosters shifting with a bigger job pool). The
over-bar Warehouse Lift is a standing problem since the standard force (#176), not caused by this change; it is the owner's call
(fewer `OPPOSITION.extra` bodies, a lower threat for police jobs, or a looser bar).

## The new jobs at their gate (best team, 24 campaigns x 100 fights)

| Job | Fight | Steady: clean win / job done / left behind | Committed: a death | Bars |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dead Air | holdExtract, Docks mix, no waves | 80% / 93% / 8% | 3.9% | within |
| Not for Sale | holdExtract, Docks mix (Basin), no waves | 80% / 93% / 7% | 4.0% | within |
| Still Connected | holdHack, corporate, 2 secforce at round 3 | 47% / 47% / 0% | 0.2% | within |
| One More Song | holdHack, corporate (Night Market, Docks leader), no waves | 43% / 43% / 0% | 1.0% | within |
| The Wrong Fare | holdExtract, corporate (Heights leader), 2 secforce at round 3 | 16% / 61% / **44%** | **32.5%** | **over both: held back** |

Read: the two Docks-mix extractions play like the old Warehouse Lift did before it had police. The corporate hacks are hard
(about half the best teams pull back on steady) but safe: nobody is left behind on a hack, and committed deaths stay near 1%.
The Wrong Fare stacks the Heights corp leader (2 pips, hits for 2), four secforce, a wave of two and the pressure clock onto a
van fight; that is Warehouse Lift's problem and worse. Levers for the owner, if it should ship: drop the wave, drop its threat to
3, or make it a holdHack.

`sim-teams` (starting rosters, real run difficulty) says the same in a harsher setting: The Wrong Fare 0% clean wins for every
team; Still Connected 3-26%; One More Song 0-46%; Dead Air and Not for Sale 20-72%.

`sim-orders` and `sim-vanlevers` are identical before and after (they play fixed jobs).
