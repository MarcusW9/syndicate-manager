# Slice 3-i: before and after (2026-11-15)

Real fight code, the same seeds and settings as the last re-baseline, run on main at #176 (`*-before-3i`) and on the slice 3-i
branch (`*-after-3i`). No job has a wave recipe yet, so what moved is the approach stage (an arrival takes no aimed shot or flank
in its first round and cannot run) and flee per group (each batch of arrivals breaks on its own; a lone arrival never runs).

Commands: `node scripts/sim-orders.mjs 100 <date>`, `sim-teams.mjs 100 <date>`, `sim-vanlevers.mjs 100 <date>`,
`sim-gate-teams.mjs 24 100 <date>`, `sim-standard-force.mjs 40 60` (printed, not saved; numbers below).

## Orders matter less than before (the validation gate)

`sim-orders` verdict: **REWORK** before and after (the gate's bar, hash `f440deff`, unchanged). After 3-i the default plan is as
good as any unchanged plan in 83% of cells (was 70%), and adapting mid-fight clears the bar against every unchanged plan in 5% of
cells (was 15%). The one plan that got worse is all-attack in van fights: left behind 11% to 30%. With flee per group, late
arrivals no longer run when the starting enemies break, so a crew that only attacks keeps fighting and misses the van.

Read: the approach stage gives every crew a free round against arrivals, which helps whatever the orders are, so the gap
between plans shrinks. The slice's tools for the player (the `approaching` priority, waves to aim it at) are not reachable yet:
no screen offers priority words and no job has a recipe. Until they are, 3-i makes fights easier without making orders matter
more. For the owner: the playtest, and which jobs get waves.
